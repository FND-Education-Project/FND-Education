## For the Person With FND

A functional tremor may change when attention or movement rhythm changes. **Distractibility** means that the tremor becomes different, smaller or briefly absent during another task. **Entrainment** means that the tremor changes toward the rhythm being produced by an unaffected hand or foot.

These are positive clinical signs because they show a pattern associated with functional tremor. They do not show that the tremor is deliberate. The person is usually trying to complete the second task, not consciously deciding what the tremor will do. (*citations* [1](#citation-1), [2](#citation-2))

A tremor can vary for many reasons. A clinician looks for a clear pattern across several tasks and considers medication, anxiety, fatigue and other movement disorders. One brief pause is not enough to diagnose functional tremor.

## For Family, Friends, and Other Supporters

Do not repeatedly distract the person, surprise them or demand that they copy rhythms to see whether the tremor changes. Diagnostic observation belongs in a clinical assessment. At home, changes in tremor may be useful information for the person’s treatment team, but should not become surveillance or a test of credibility.

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Technique outline: distractibility and entrainment

1. Record the tremor at rest, in posture and during the movement that usually brings it out. Keep the camera angle and limb position stable.
2. Ask the patient to perform a separate, clearly explained task with an unaffected limb. Appropriate tasks may include a brief ballistic movement or rhythmic finger or foot tapping.
3. For entrainment, ask the unaffected limb to tap at one slow rhythm, then at one or two distinctly different rhythms. A metronome may help, but should not be uncomfortably loud.
4. Observe whether the original tremor changes frequency toward the imposed rhythm, pauses, becomes less regular, changes amplitude or makes the tapping task unusually difficult.
5. Repeat only enough to establish whether the response is reproducible. Avoid exhausting the patient or intensifying symptoms for demonstration.
6. Interpret the result with tremor history, distribution, activation conditions and the rest of the movement examination. Electrophysiological testing may support uncertain or complex cases.

Functional tremor commonly shows variability, distractibility and entrainability, but no one feature is infallible. The examiner should also consider coexisting functional and non-functional tremors. (*citations* [1](#citation-1), [2](#citation-2))

### Media contributor brief

Use one continuous medium-width shot that includes the tremulous limb, the tapping limb and the clinician’s instructions. Add a small on-screen rhythm label—such as “slow,” “medium” and “fast”—and preserve the original audio so viewers can match the tapping to any tremor change.

The video should contain:

1. Ten to fifteen seconds of baseline tremor.
2. A distraction task that does not involve startling the participant.
3. At least two clearly different tapping rhythms.
4. A replay only after the uninterrupted sequence has been shown.
5. A clinician explanation of the observed change and its limitations.

Do not use rapid cuts, speed changes or music over the examination. Those edits would make frequency comparison unreliable. Captions should describe the task rather than declare that the sign “proves FND.”
