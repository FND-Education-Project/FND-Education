# Functional visual symptoms: evidence-presentation review

October 10, 2026. Internal editorial record; excluded from the website and search. Overview, three detailed pages and thirteen-entry inventory reviewed; not a systematic review or clinical approval.

## Lead evidence and link decisions

- Ramsay 2024 (FND-CIT-0024): published full text accessed through the University of Edinburgh repository. Retain as lead clinical guidance on positive demonstration, explanation and coexistence. A clinical framework is not validation of every optical comparison. No exact acuity is inferred from an optokinetic response.
- Ramsay 2026 (0103): full text accessed through PMC. Review of 44 studies/2,284 participants, search through October 2024. Supports coexistence and distinguishing symptomatic disability from examination signs. Not a diagnostic-accuracy meta-analysis; added to inventory as supporting context, without adding an inventory entry. Its recency does not eliminate weaknesses in the underlying largely retrospective literature.
- Raviskanthan 2022 (0179): full text inspected. Useful optical descriptions coexist with broad assertions about movement/obstacle behaviour and a sunglasses sign (observation section), and terminology grouping that can blur the distinction between FND and intentional symptom production. It explicitly rejects shock/menace tests, which is a strength; that does not resolve the behaviour-based framing. Remove external paper and central-ID hyperlinks on all four public pages citing it; retain exact bibliography and stable ID as plain text, and one internal reading link. Current positive clinical explanation precedes its supporting citation. Do not reproduce its behavioural stereotypes, acuity shortcuts or suggested shock demonstrations.
- Scott–Egan 2003 (0180): primary abstract rechecked. Retain scoped coexistence finding (71/133) with selected referral-population limit. Full-methods appraisal remains outstanding; no claim of complete full-text clearance. Older terminology alone does not justify unlinking a study that supports checking for coexisting disease.
- NHS vision-loss guidance (0104): existing symptom-specific urgent-care boundary retained.

## Newer candidate: not adopted as a clinical rule

Na H, Hwang JM, Yang HK, Han SB. Characterizing Visual Field Defects with Tangent Screen Perimetry in Organic Versus Non-Organic Pathologies. Diagnostics. 2026;16(6):842. DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16060842. Institutional abstract and primary indexed text checked. Retrospective 2009–2019 data, 126 eyes/76 patients selected for constriction within 30 degrees, with overlapping tangent ratios. Indexed figure text labels a patient as malingering; this requires checking how intentional symptoms were classified and separated from functional symptoms. Full publisher/PMC retrieval was unavailable. Keep as an internal candidate, not a newly validated FND cutoff or public recommendation. One paper-access link is in the reading guide.

Searches on October 10 covered 2025–2026 functional visual diagnosis, reviews, field testing and current Ramsay work. A five-patient virtual-reality perimetry conference abstract and a 2026 editorial were also located; neither replaces the current clinical framework. No claim of exhaustive retrieval.
