# Functional facial symptoms evidence-presentation review

Internal editorial record, October 11, 2026. Covers the symptom overview, three detailed guides and seven-entry inventory. Human, movement-disorders, neuro-ophthalmology, oral medicine, SLT and accessibility review remain pending.

## Clinical frame and source decisions

Lead with involuntary symptoms, positive pattern recognition and assessment for alternative or coexisting conditions. Distinguish muscle overactivity from weakness; retain acute stroke assessment and eye-protection boundaries. Preserve diagnosis versus everyday function, without adding a public warning to each historical citation.

- Frucht 2021 (0021) and Stone's facial guidance (0056) lead the explanation. Relevant facial/oromandibular sections checked; Stone diagnostic sections rechecked October 11. These are review/expert guidance, not new validation. Frucht added to the jaw/tongue guide; existing source ID reused.
- Fasano 2012 (0052): exact bibliography, DOI and ID remain plain text on reviewed public pages, with the original link retained once in the internal reading guide. This is based on substantive framing, not the title's age: indexed primary text includes normal neurological examination, somatization and response to suggestion/psychotherapy in its diagnostic summary, and groups intentional presentations among historical diagnostic categories. Retain descriptive observations from the 61-person retrospective series, without importing these assumptions. Earlier full-text review is recorded in the expansion guide; this pass rechecked indexed publisher/PMC passages. Direct PMC retrieval was blocked and the repository PDF failed, so no claim of a new complete full-text appraisal.
- Yoshida 2020 (0053): plain-text public bibliography and ID, one internal access link. Publisher full text checked. Its positive-diagnosis discussion is useful, but selection uses a study-specific greater-than-seven-of-ten rule incorporating placebo/non-physiological manoeuvres and other nonspecific features. This can mislead if read as a clinical diagnostic recipe. Retain selected-cohort findings and their selection limits; use explained, consented clinical comparisons in the public workflow. The decision is not based on psychological content or publication age. No study treatment protocol adopted.
- Schwingenschuh 2011 (0124): retain the link and tightly scoped specialist-adjunct discussion. Primary indexed methods/results checked: 10 essential, nine atypical/presumed functional and nine controls. This is not validation of a normal result as proof of FND. Historical terminology alone is not a reason to unlink. Complete full-text framing/method appraisal and replication review remain pending; direct PMC retrieval was blocked. Public access-status sentence moved here.
- Horn 2017 (0155): preserve the distinction between its unilateral limb-weakness population and isolated facial symptoms. No facial accuracy estimates imported.
- Popkirov 2020 (0054), Malaty 2022 (0110), and NHS sources (0108, 0197) retain their existing bounded roles: stroke mimic explanation, tic differential and safety guidance respectively. No new claims added from these sources.

## Scope and follow-up

Targeted searches for newer facial diagnostic studies did not identify a stronger general validation study to replace the descriptive cohorts. This is not proof that none exists. The 2016 facial/tongue chapter repeats much of the older diagnostic framing; it is not adopted merely because it is newer. Full appraisal of the small blink-reflex study and any later replication remains a specific follow-up.
