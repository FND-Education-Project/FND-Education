# Functional tic-like symptoms evidence-presentation review

Internal editorial record, October 12, 2026. Covers the overview, three detailed guides and nine-entry inventory. Human, tic-specialist, lived-experience and accessibility review remain pending.

## Clinical framework

Retain involuntary symptoms, motor and vocal assessment, developmental history and possible primary-tic coexistence. Demographics, psychiatric diagnoses, symptom content and social-media exposure cannot individually settle diagnosis. Measurement of symptoms, intended communication, participation and assistance remain separate.

## Sources and currentness

- Malaty 2022 (0110), ESSTS consensus 2023 (0111) and Szejko assessment guidance (0114) retain their scoped roles. Direct Malaty publisher retrieval returned a rate-limit error in this pass; inherited source appraisal is not represented as a new full-text review. ESSTS remains consensus at publication, not prospective accuracy validation. Szejko measures concern primary tic disorders; no FTLB-specific threshold is invented.
- Nilles 2024 (0264): primary indexed methods/results rechecked against the prior full-text review. The two primary-tic registry cohorts address selected-feature specificity, not sensitivity or complete framework accuracy. Add the existing citation directly to the inventory, alongside the original consensus and critique.
- Andersen 2024 (0112) remains a methodological critique, not grounds to dismiss all functional diagnoses. Complete criteria-table and critique appraisal remain follow-up tasks from the earlier expansion.
- Cavanna 2025 (0172) remains evidence about mixed presentations, not independent validation of criteria used in selecting cases. Treatment reports and neurophysiology sources retain their existing bounded roles. Treatment response does not retrospectively establish diagnosis.
- Gary, Shaw and McGuire, October 2, 2026 (new 0273): publisher methods, bias appraisal, results and discussion checked. Synthesis of 39 studies; search updated June 21, 2026. It is a systematic narrative synthesis, not meta-analysis or prospective validation of the complete diagnostic framework. Most included evidence is descriptive/cross-sectional, with overlapping clinical samples; most quality ratings were initially completed by one reviewer, with a subset independently rated.

## Presentation decision for the new review

Use Gary's relevant synthesis in the overview, criteria guide and inventory, with exact plain-text bibliography, DOI and ID. Keep one original-paper link in the internal reading guide. Its introduction frames historical disorders broadly through psychosomatic/social-contagion explanations; its discussion also invokes intentional or unintentional secondary gain. These explanations must not be transferred into a person's diagnosis or motive. The public text leads with involuntary symptoms and possible coexistence, without reproducing the objectionable wording or adding repeated warnings. Recent publication alone does not resolve framing concerns.

Targeted searches also found a 2026 cross-sectional child-service mental-health comparison and a conference description of an ESSTS-derived tool. Neither is adopted as full independent diagnostic validation: the former concerns group associations, and the latter requires a complete peer-reviewed methods/results appraisal. No new diagnostic calculator, threshold or demographic rule is introduced.

All nine entries, three Quick Reference links, headings, share anchors and media briefs retained. Public appraisal-status notes moved internally and existing numeric citations made consistent with project display style. No personal accounts restored.
