# Functional drop attacks evidence-presentation review

Internal editorial record, October 12, 2026. Covers the overview, three detailed guides and seven-entry inventory. Targeted review; human and specialist appraisal remain pending.

## Decisions and access

- Add Manford, Garg and Manford 2024 (0272), *Drop attacks: a practical guide*, to the overview, event-history guide, differential guide and inventory. Primary PubMed abstract, authors and publication metadata checked; publisher retrieval failed. Only its broad differential scope and variable use of “drop attack” are added. Full-text appraisal remains open; no detailed testing protocol or diagnostic accuracy is inferred from the abstract.
- Hoeritzauer 2018 (0059) remains the descriptive functional-overlap cohort. Earlier review recorded 83 patients assessed by one clinician. No new full-text appraisal claimed: publisher retrieval failed. Its overlap findings do not make every unexplained fall functional or validate an authored event record.
- Revell 2021 (0060) remains qualitative model-building evidence. Primary indexed record and institutional full-text record located; earlier expansion full-text appraisal retained. Seven participants and limited diary contribution do not establish universal cause or treatment effect. Keep the distinction between an authored activity record and a validated measure.
- Stone's functional-drop-attack page (0061) rechecked as specialist education, not independent validation.
- NICE CG109 (0263): current indexed official recommendations rechecked, including initial 12-lead ECG and the 2023 amendment. Full recommendation-page retrieval again failed. Its over-16 suspected-blackout pathway remains separate from functional-drop-attack diagnosis. No new ECG, tilt or standing protocol added.

## Currentness and scope

Targeted 2025–2026 searches identified later discussion of uncommon structural causes, but no stronger functional-drop-attack validation that displaces these sources. The broad clinical review complements rather than replaces the functional cohort. Keep incomplete recall distinct from confirmed preserved awareness, and keep cardiovascular, vestibular, sleep, epilepsy and mechanical possibilities visible.

All seven entries, three Quick Reference links, headings, share anchors, safety boundaries and media briefs retained. Appraisal/access notes are internal, while the public limitations explain how to use the evidence. No archived human accounts restored. Review of broader recovery protocols is outside this diagnostic pass.
