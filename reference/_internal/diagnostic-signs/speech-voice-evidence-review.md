# Speech and voice: evidence-presentation review

October 10, 2026. Internal editorial record; excluded from the website and search. Overview, three detailed pages and eight-entry inventory reviewed. Targeted review, not a systematic review or clinical approval.

## Source decisions and access

- Baker 2021 (FND-CIT-0025) remains the principal specialist framework. The 69-page long supplementary version deposited at UCL was read for positive diagnosis, speech/voice assessment and swallowing. Consensus recommendations are not a validated diagnostic battery. Psychological causation is not required; coexisting disease remains possible.
- Chung 2018 (0169): publisher abstract and video captions checked. The abstract presumes psychological causation and captions use exaggerated/childlike framing. Retain descriptive observations and exact bibliography, DOI, PMID and stable ID as plain text on all five public speech pages. Remove paper and central-index hyperlinks there. The internal reading guide retains one original-paper link. Six cases cannot establish generalizable diagnostic accuracy. Full body/methods not newly appraised.
- Freeburn 2025 (0246): primary indexed abstract rechecked. Thirty functional-speech participants and 47 healthy controls; AUC 0.98 is discrimination in this pilot, not 98% clinical diagnostic accuracy. Neurological comparators and independent validation remain necessary. Full methods not available; access bookkeeping moved off the public page.
- ASHA Voice and Fluency guidance checked directly. Fluency explicitly excludes functional and acquired neurogenic disorders; retain only adjacent assessment/participation use. AAC retrieval returned 403; existing communication-access guidance retained without claiming fresh full-text appraisal. Stroke safety and tic differential sources retained in their existing scope.

## Currentness search

Targeted October 10 searches covered functional speech/voice diagnosis and 2023–2026 studies. Freeburn and Baker's 2023 review (10.1016/j.ncl.2023.02.005) was identified; institutional abstract and metadata checked, full text unavailable. It does not establish a newly validated test and is an internal follow-up candidate, not a substitute for the specialist consensus. Newer developmental speech literature using similar terminology was not treated as adult FND evidence.

Positive clinical features lead the explanation. Preserve task-specific interpretation, laryngeal differential assessment, AAC access and the distinction between speech performance and cognition.
