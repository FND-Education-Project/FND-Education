# Functional sensory symptoms: evidence-presentation review

October 10, 2026. Internal editorial record; excluded from the website and search. Targeted evidence-presentation review of the overview, two detailed pages and seven-entry inventory; not a systematic review or clinical approval.

## Decisions and sources

- Nielsen 2026 (FND-CIT-0023) remains the lead comparative evidence. Publisher full text, methods, Table 4 and discussion checked. Distinguish patient-reported midline boundaries from dense examination findings and partial vibration asymmetry. The latter was more frequent in motor-FND but also occurred after stroke. Do not collapse these into one accuracy estimate. Table 4 excludes eight stroke participants with a positive Hoover sign; the relevant comparison denominator is 67, not all 75 stroke participants. No new numerical test threshold is introduced.
- The study enrolled established motor-FND, excluded several comorbid/access groups and differs from stroke in age and illness duration. It does not validate diagnosis or prognosis in isolated sensory presentations. Existing disability and follow-up claims retained within their population.
- Chabrol 1995 (0126): publisher summary checked: 40 neurological and 15 conversion-disorder participants, overlap across traditional signs. Retain the link: the available report challenges diagnostic stereotypes rather than supporting them. Full sensory-specific methods remain unavailable; no new accuracy estimate.
- Daum 2015 (0130): retain as mixed motor/sensory/gait pilot evidence, not sensory-only accuracy. Full-text reappraisal remains outstanding.
- Urman 2026 (0244): primary abstract rechecked, 55/194 neurological diagnoses. Full methods not accessed. Unexplained cases are not reclassified as FND.
- Koh 2021 (0243), MSD examination guidance (0245), Espay 2018 (0002) and IFCN 2024 (0022): retained in their existing scope. Earlier expansion appraisal remains in project history. The motor neurophysiology chapter is background, not validation of sensory investigations.

## Previous expansion access notes

### 07-functional-sensory-symptoms

In a 2026 retrospective emergency-department series, a neurological diagnosis was identified in 55 of 194 people with hemisensory complaints, including stroke/TIA, migraine and multiple sclerosis. The study does not establish that the remaining patients had FND. Its abstract was reviewed; the full paper was not available. (*citations* 7)
