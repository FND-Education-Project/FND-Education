## Audience-by-audience editorial assessment

- **Person:** Lesson 30 teaches competing demands (Natalie's unexpected pharmacy/grocery overlap; Jamie's photo deadline and multiple questions), with working-memory, executive-function, salience, interoception, retrieval and feedback-loop definitions applied after the events rather than dumped upfront. Lesson 31 advances to decisions with incomplete information (Priya's valued café outing, followed by an uncertain difficult morning; Daniel's delayed, prolonged change after physical and cognitive tasks). Lesson 32 asks whether treatment gains are usable in daily life (Natalie's accessible physiotherapy and other weekly needs; Amira's accurate drawing versus accessible creative work). All three teach uncertainty without blaming the patient, incorporate humour and value enjoyment despite symptoms. Reader decision pauses are engaging and answer keys resist a single “right” interpretation. **The missing conventional three-part reflection endings are an explicit owner-format decision, not an automatic error.** **Priya's apparent identity conflict is the principal substantive continuity question.**
- **Supporter:** The single connected Owen/Maya/Pip scene per lesson is an **approved supersession** of an earlier two-story plan. Lesson 30 makes shared household responsibilities visible when Maya is sick without assigning causation for Owen's familiar seizure. Lesson 31 portrays both Owen's agency and Maya's legitimate limits in a thwarted painting trip; offers planning without presuming hindsight makes all disappointment preventable. Lesson 32 balances ordinary comfort, requests, delayed responses, repair after irritability, supporter rest and shared pleasure. The dog contributes responsibilities and companionship without becoming a gimmick. The relaxed advice-over-coffee voice works. **Lesson 32 still requires explicit owner approval.**
- **Clinician:** Lesson 30 uses genuinely different reasoning cases: Morgan's gait-plus-communication task and brief visual dimming during standing, versus David's prolonged functional seizure during a migraine consultation, the burden of examination, retained experience and incomplete recovery/follow-up. Lesson 31 covers Evan's postoperative knee improvement with persisting muscle loss, variable FND symptoms and accessible supervision, versus Jonah's delayed disproportionate worsening, recent illness and suspected PEM with medical co-assessment. These cases ask for observations, missing information, distinctions and defensible decisions. Lesson 32 deliberately moves from case exercises to symptom-specific evidence and individualized care, rather than reteaching neuroplasticity. Avoid making general rehabilitation rules from a single fictional outcome or from a different medical condition.
- **Across audiences:** The progression **identify demands → revise decisions with new information → reconcile treatment with meaningful daily life** is coherent. Concepts build on Module 9 and earlier lessons without another full neurological explanation. Distinctions between performance, sustainable participation and treatment relevance are kept. Safety messaging is generally proportionate: FND does not explain every new symptom; coexisting migraine, posture-related problems, and possibly PEM deserve assessment; support is consent-based and neither partner is a substitute clinician.
