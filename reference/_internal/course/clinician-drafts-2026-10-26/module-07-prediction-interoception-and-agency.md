# Proposed Module 7 — Prediction, Body Signals, and Agency
**For Clinicians and the Care Team — proposed Lessons 18–21**  
**Status:** Internal clinical editorial draft, not publication-ready. Pair with [Person drafts](../person-drafts-2026-10-26/module-07-prediction-interoception-and-agency.md). Supporter sections deliberately unplanned.

The purpose is not to supply a complete causal theory of FND. It is to explain what the constructs mean, what has or has not been measured, and how clinicians can **formulate a testable, patient-benefiting adaptation** while preserving differential diagnosis, access and clinical accountability. Any application below is illustrative unless a phenotype-specific source is cited.

# Lesson 18 — The Predictive Brain

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Models illuminate hypotheses; they do not certify causes

Predictive-processing accounts describe the interaction of expectations, incoming sensory evidence and estimates of their reliability. In these models, a **prior** is not necessarily a consciously held belief, a **prediction error** is a mismatch rather than a patient's mistake, and **precision weighting** is computationally different from confidence or willpower. These constructs can be helpful for generating hypotheses about perception and action in FND, but evidence for a patient-specific altered prior cannot be read straight from persistent symptoms, normal structural imaging or therapeutic response.

Take a clinician who notices that an activity becomes harder when timing is uncertain. A useful formulation may include anticipation, motor planning, fatigue, environmental complexity and other disease. The legitimate next step is to clarify task conditions and consider a reversible adjustment—not to tell the person they expected failure and created it.

**Physiotherapists** may use predictable cues or a clear task target as clinically indicated while tracking function and adverse effects. **OT** may examine whether scheduling, physical setup or step order reduces uncertainty in valued activities. **Psychologists** can explore expectation, self-efficacy and distress when the person identifies them as relevant, without converting a computational theory into blame.

**Symptom application — Functional speech and voice symptoms:** Speech-language clinicians may compare spontaneous, rehearsed, automatic and goal-directed speaking under appropriate assessment conditions and offer supported communication, pacing and task-specific practice. The variability of speech across tasks is not proof that a person could speak fluently on demand, and the prediction model does not establish the cause of dysphonia, stuttering or word blocking. [Speech/voice assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/09-functional-speech-and-voice-symptoms.md) · [speech/voice recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/09-functional-speech-and-voice-symptoms.md).

### Turn a theoretical explanation into a respectful conversation

A clinician can say: “Your brain constantly anticipates and adjusts. Researchers are exploring whether changes in those processes contribute to some functional symptoms. We can use that idea to consider helpful task conditions, without claiming we know why yours started.”

**Evidence boundary:** Bayesian FND accounts are theoretical; studies of speech/voice intervention cannot automatically establish the proposed computational mechanism. [R03](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r03) · [R04](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r04) · [R14](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r14). Historical diagnostic terms in original paper titles are not suitable explanatory language for a patient.

# Lesson 19 — Interoception, Proprioception, and Outside Senses

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Separate sensing, appraisal and measurement

**Interoception** includes central representation and integration of bodily conditions, with processes both within and outside awareness; **proprioception** includes position, movement and force-related sensing; **exteroception** concerns outside sensory input. “Noticing,” self-reported sensitivity, confidence, accuracy on a test, physiological status and a neural response measure are not interchangeable.

The 2026 cross-sectional cardiac interoception study in a mixed-FND cohort reported group differences in some self-report and heartbeat-evoked EEG measures, while a heartbeat-counting accuracy comparison was not statistically significant. The selected method and sample cannot diagnose a person, establish a global interoceptive deficit or prescribe treatment. A nonsignificant accuracy comparison is not evidence of equivalence.

**OT** can ask which sensations and environmental demands interfere with specific tasks and modify lighting, tactile inputs, instructions or seating. **Physiotherapy** can integrate assessed proprioceptive demands and balance safety into functional practice. **Psychology** may help reduce distress around symptoms or evaluate interpretation when relevant, but must not invalidate physiological findings. **Speech-language therapy** should consider eating, swallowing and airway complaints on their own medical safety pathway.

### Practice applications with limits

**Functional sensory symptoms:** Describe distribution, timing, variability, disability and positive signs *in context*. Where a sensory rehabilitation approach is appropriate, set tolerable goals; do not use prolonged or painful stimulation to “prove” sensation is present. [Assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/07-functional-sensory-symptoms.md) · [care](../../../recovery-techniques/07-functional-sensory-symptoms.md).

**Functional visual symptoms:** Ophthalmic and neurological assessment remains essential to evaluate ocular, neurological and coexisting causes. OT may support contrast, reading access, lighting and environmental adaptations, while specialist rehabilitation is tailored to the assessed impairment. Photophobia or visual overload is not itself proof of functional visual loss. [Assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/08-functional-visual-symptoms.md) · [care](../../../recovery-techniques/08-functional-visual-symptoms.md).

**Persistent postural-perceptual dizziness (PPPD):** Evaluate the symptom's timing, upright and motion-related context, visual sensitivity, relevant vestibular criteria and overlapping migraine, dysautonomia and other disorders. When indicated, vestibular therapists can build an individualized programme; OT can modify visually demanding environments. Avoid using “visual dependence” as a substitute for diagnosing dizziness. [Assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/13-persistent-postural-perceptual-dizziness.md) · [care](../../../recovery-techniques/13-persistent-postural-perceptual-dizziness.md).

**Functional swallowing symptoms and globus:** An altered sensation of swallowing does not rule out unsafe swallowing, aspiration, gastrointestinal or structural disease. Speech-language clinicians should assess swallowing safety and consider instrumental studies where appropriate before any retraining; psychological or attention-based approaches are not an alternative to managing airway and nutrition risk. [Assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/10-functional-swallowing-and-globus.md) · [care](../../../recovery-techniques/10-functional-swallowing-and-globus.md).

### What to record

Document one meaningful everyday goal, separate symptom description from objective measures, state uncertainty and obtain a review of the *real-world result*. Never transfer heartbeat counting, breath holding, eyes-closed balance or other research procedures to home practice.

**Evidence boundary:** Definitions are grounded in general neuroscience [R05](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r05), [R06](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r06); mixed-FND interoception research [R07](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r07) does not establish treatment efficacy. Swallowing and communication management is primarily specialist consensus [R14](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r14).

# Lesson 20 — Salience, Arousal, and Feedback Loops

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Map interactions without assigning blame

Salience denotes relevance or processing priority; a reassuring voice may be highly salient without being threatening. Arousal includes physiological and cognitive readiness and is not equivalent to anxiety or trauma. A feedback loop is a proposed interaction, not a demonstrated direction of causation. Disability itself may generate vigilance, distress and increased arousal; so can pain, loss of sleep, medication effects and another active disease.

A clinician who notices more episodes during crowded appointments can address noise, waiting, communication, positioning or access without claiming that environmental stress *caused* the episodes. Ask the person what mattered and distinguish an observed association from an explanatory model.

**Neurology/emergency medicine:** assess events on their own merits, including injury, breathing, atypical duration and medical instability. **Psychology:** where acceptable, support coping with uncertainty, fear, social consequences or symptom-related distress; do not insist on trauma or a single emotion-based cause. **OT/PT:** adapt hazards, transitions and environmental demands. **SLT/respiratory/ENT teams:** address breathing or upper-airway safety directly before considering any pattern-focused management.

### Apply across episodic presentations

**Functional seizures:** Some people notice a warning or context, others none. Offer event-specific diagnosis, clinician-reviewed safety plans, appropriate psychological interventions by choice and ongoing care. Do not propose universal arousal control, forced grounding, benzodiazepines for functional seizures without another indication, or self-induction of an event. Assess coexisting epilepsy and other causes. [Seizure assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/06-functional-seizures.md) · [recovery/safety](../../../recovery-techniques/06-functional-seizures.md).

**Functional drop attacks:** When no usable warning exists, immediate injury protection, safe mobility, floor recovery and medical differential evaluation take priority over warning-based self-interruption. Consider syncope, cardiac and other fall mechanisms as clinically indicated. [Drop attack assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/16-functional-drop-attacks.md) · [drop attack care](../../../recovery-techniques/16-functional-drop-attacks.md).

**Functional cough and upper-airway symptoms:** Clarify the cough/voice/breathing phenotype and competing respiratory, laryngeal, medication and other causes. An SLP-led approach to a *diagnosed* functional presentation may involve individualized cough or laryngeal control work; it is not a reason to defer urgent airway assessment or prescribe unsupervised breath-holding. [Assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/11-functional-cough-and-upper-airway-symptoms.md) · [recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/11-functional-cough-and-upper-airway-symptoms.md).

**Coexisting autonomic complaints:** Palpitations, orthostatic faintness, sweating, bowel changes or temperature symptoms require appropriate assessment for cardiac, endocrine, medication-related, dysautonomic and other contributors. “Arousal” does not diagnose their cause. See the [autonomic overlap reference](../../../co-occurring-conditions/08-autonomic-symptoms.md).

### How to use an interaction map

Make a short, revisable hypothesis: “During a long, noisy appointment, pain and fatigue might reduce communication capacity.” Offer one low-risk adjustment and assess whether it improves access. A worsening or unexpected sign invites reassessment, not an assumption of resistance.

**Evidence boundary:** Salience network studies in general populations [R08](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r08) and integrated FND frameworks [R04](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r04) do not establish an individual autonomic mechanism. Functional-seizure care must follow phenotype-specific guidance [R11](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r11).

# Lesson 21 — Agency, Learning, and Personal Adaptations

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Separate intention, experience and actual control

The **sense of agency** concerns the experience of initiating or controlling an action. It is distinct from intention, responsibility, body ownership, voluntary effort and the reliable ability to repeat an action. Explicit self-report, intentional-binding paradigms, imaging and naturalistic daily performance are different measures and cannot be substituted for one another.

Small motor-FND imaging studies have examined agency-related processes. They are not validity tests, biomarkers or evidence that patients lack insight. Describe agency findings as emerging research, not an explanation that can be assigned from behaviour alone.

### What would an adaptation look like in each profession?

**Physiotherapy:** Let a person identify a valued movement and decide whether a clinician-guided task change improves consistency or reduces risk.

**Occupational therapy:** Use task analysis to identify where intended action and accessible execution diverge; offer a tool, positioning change, task sequence or alternative method while protecting autonomy.

**Psychology/neuropsychology:** Work with loss of confidence, dissociation, identity or the effects of unpredictable control *where present*, not as required causes or prerequisites for care.

**Speech-language therapy:** A person may intend to speak but lose voice or fluency; support their chosen communication channel without making spontaneous speech a test of credibility.

### A shared reasoning method

A concise clinical sequence is: **define the task → ask what the person experienced → check alternative/coexisting medical explanations → agree on one safe adjustment → review reliability and life impact**. A positive response is useful for care, not proof of the proposed brain mechanism; an absent response does not invalidate the diagnosis.

In shared discussion, say “the symptom is involuntary; we can work with the conditions in which the activity is possible” rather than “your brain is making you do it because you expected it.”

**Evidence boundary:** Agency imaging is limited and phenotype-specific [R09](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r09). Broader circuit frameworks [R04](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r04) remain proposals. Human review should assess whether this section does enough to preserve dignity without diluting the science.
