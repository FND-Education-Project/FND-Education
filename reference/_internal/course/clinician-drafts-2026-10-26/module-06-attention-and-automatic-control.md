The clinical task in this module is to turn a useful observation about attention or automaticity into an **individualized, consent-based question for assessment or rehabilitation**—not to presume that attention causes every symptom. The following clinical examples are proposed applications of relevant consensus and phenotype-specific research, not verified standalone treatments. # Lesson 15 — Attention: What the Brain Notices

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Treat attention as a set of functions, not a single dial

A person's performance may depend on selective, sustained and shifting attention, the demands of the environment and simultaneous bodily or cognitive tasks. Attentional capture by pain, intrusive sensory input or symptom-related concern is different from a conscious decision to focus on symptoms. Similarly, reducing demands can improve task performance without proving that attention produced the underlying condition.

**Occupational therapists** can begin with a concrete task—reading appointment instructions, preparing a meal or participating in a noisy clinic. Examine visual complexity, interruptions, instructions, motor demands, time pressure and sensory access. Offer one adjustment, such as a written cue, fewer simultaneous steps or a predictable workspace, and agree whether improved accuracy, effort or participation constitutes a meaningful result.

**Neuropsychologists and psychologists** can distinguish attentional load from memory encoding, dissociation, anxiety or threat interpretations. A brief formal test in a quiet room is not equivalent to managing everyday competing demands; routine supports and separate assessment of sleep, pain, migraine, medication effects and other contributors remain appropriate.

**Speech-language therapists** can adapt conversational turn-taking, response time, written prompts and communication access when language, word-finding or voice symptoms occur under load. **Neurologists** should describe which observed changes informed the diagnosis, without implying that an attentional improvement establishes the mechanism.

**Symptom application — Functional Cognitive Disorder (FCD):** A person may lose the next instruction while managing several tasks. Consider attention and encoding demands, functional inconsistency in its clinical context, separate cognitive diagnoses and patient-important activities. Trial a single capture method or task-sequencing support rather than repeated memory testing. [FCD diagnostic overview](../../../diagnostic-signs/12-functional-cognitive-disorder.md) · [FCD recovery overview](../../../recovery-techniques/12-functional-cognitive-disorder.md).

### Close the loop without creating surveillance

Agree on an observable target (for example, one form section completed accurately with less fatigue), a low-burden adaptation and a review date. Ask whether the change made the *task* easier, not whether the patient “stopped paying attention” to a symptom. A worsening outcome may reflect extra divided attention or unsuitable cues; withdrawing accommodations is not a therapeutic default.

**Evidence boundary:** Goal-directed/stimulus-driven attention comes from general neuroscience. FND-specific attention findings—including selected action-tremor research—are narrower than this overall framework. # Lesson 16 — Automatic Control and Conscious Effort

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Separate “can happen once” from reliable function

Motor assessment and treatment should distinguish the **capacity demonstrated in one condition**, **ability to initiate on request**, **duration or reproducibility**, and **everyday safety and participation**. Positive FND signs have significance within a professional examination; successful movement during a task does not demonstrate safe independent walking, transfers, self-care or freedom from coexisting disease.

**Physiotherapists** may contrast an assessed isolated movement with a meaningful functional task and use goal-oriented or externally directed cues when appropriate. Set up fall protection before testing. **Occupational therapists** can translate any preserved movement into an achievable activity, while considering grip, environment, cognitive load, seating, support needs and whether the result survives fatigue. **Psychologists** may address distress, avoidance, loss of confidence and treatment engagement when relevant, without using behavioural theory to override physical findings.

**Symptom application — Functional limb weakness:** Where positive examination signs support the diagnosis, consider task-oriented retraining and accessible upper- or lower-limb activities in collaboration with PT and OT. Do not turn a Hoover-sign response into a conclusion about household function. [Weakness assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/01-functional-limb-weakness.md) · [task-oriented recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/functional_limb_weakness/01-automatic-and-task-oriented-movement.md).

**Symptom application — Functional paralysis:** With severe immobility, assess positioning, skin care, transfers and a sustainable support plan before or alongside clinician-guided practice. A moment of motion does not eliminate the need for equipment or assistance. [Paralysis assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/15-functional-paralysis.md) · [paralysis care options](../../../recovery-techniques/15-functional-paralysis.md).

**Symptom application — Functional gait disorder:** Task context, direction changes, visual targets or rhythm may be explored *only in an appropriately assessed, protected setting*. Translate any task-specific gain into actual walking safety, confidence and participation, not just clinic-step quality. [Gait assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/05-functional-gait-disorder.md) · [gait recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/05-functional-gait-disorder.md).

**Symptom application — Functional facial symptoms:** Some eye, jaw or facial movements vary with task and attention. Assess relevant neurological, ocular, dental and swallowing issues. PT/OT or speech-language therapy adaptations must be matched to the facial pattern rather than assuming that stronger effort or imitation is corrective. [Facial assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/14-functional-facial-symptoms.md) · [facial recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/14-functional-facial-symptoms.md).

### Explain a useful finding without implying choice

A constructive explanation is: “Your movement changed when the task changed. This helps us understand the pattern and gives us possible rehabilitation approaches. It does not mean you chose the difficulty, and we'll still plan around what is safe and reliable for you.”

**Evidence boundary:** Motor task-retraining recommendations are primarily professional consensus; the task-dependent attention experiment covers selected functional action tremor. Neither justifies universal claims for weakness, paralysis, gait or facial symptoms. [R01](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r01) · [R02](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r02).

# Lesson 17 — Distraction, External Focus, and Context

## For Clinicians and the Care Team

### Use the correct term for the intervention

**Distractibility** is an examination observation when symptom expression changes while attention is engaged elsewhere. **Attention redirection** is an intentionally selected strategy; **external focus** refers to attending to an action's target or effect. **Entrainment** means a movement rhythm is drawn toward a requested competing rhythm. Merely stopping, pausing or suppressing a movement is not entrainment. Adding a dual task may increase risk or overload and should not be equated with simplifying instructions.

**Physiotherapy** can select a meaningful, safe task target or rhythm when its specific rationale is relevant. **OT** can compare instruction formats, environmental demands and the actual task outcome. **Psychology** can examine anticipation, fear of injury and stress-related burden where identified, while avoiding pressure to distract away from medical warning signs. Document the strategy, baseline context, patient preference, intended effect and any adverse responses.

**Symptom application — Functional tremor:** Specialist assessment may demonstrate distractibility or entrainment in an appropriate setting. Rehabilitation could trial a functional task or externally focused cue, documenting hand use, fatigue and interference rather than symptom suppression alone. [Tremor examination](../../../diagnostic-signs/02-functional-tremor.md) · [task-focused recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/functional_tremor/03-external-focus-and-meaningful-task-practice.md).

**Symptom application — Functional jerks/myoclonus:** Establish warning availability, falls, medication and other causes before discussing attention-based redirection. A no-warning jerk may require injury-prevention and environmental adaptations rather than a cue the person cannot use in time. [Jerks assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/03-functional-jerks-and-myoclonus.md) · [jerks care](../../../recovery-techniques/03-functional-jerks-and-myoclonus.md).

**Symptom application — Functional tics and tic-like symptoms:** Clarify phenomenology, onset, motor/vocal features, possible premonitory sensations and differential diagnoses. Behavioural treatment principles derived from tic disorders must not be presented as proven equivalent for all functional tic-like presentations. Combine suitable care with dignity, school/work access and avoidance of punitive response training. [Tic assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/17-functional-tics-and-tic-like-symptoms.md) · [tic recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/17-functional-tics-and-tic-like-symptoms.md).

**Symptom application — Functional dystonia/fixed posturing:** Painful or sustained posturing may not respond to distraction or simple rhythm. Prioritize complications, non-forceful positioning, task suitability and specialist coordination; do not force a fixed posture toward normal alignment. [Dystonia assessment](../../../diagnostic-signs/04-functional-dystonia.md) · [dystonia recovery](../../../recovery-techniques/04-functional-dystonia.md).

### What would make the trial worthwhile?

Use a negotiated outcome such as safer utensil use, reduced interference during a chosen activity, less pain or fewer injuries. Stop or modify an unsuitable cue. No response—or a response in the opposite direction—neither confirms nor refutes FND.

**Evidence boundary:** FND motor rehabilitation consensus and selected tremor experiments do not establish that distraction is a universal treatment or that a given manipulation demonstrates malingering. [R01](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r01) · [R02](../new-lessons-research-2026-10-26/sources.md#r02).
