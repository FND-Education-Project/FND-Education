# Research brief — proposed Lesson 30: Balancing Rehabilitation, Rest, and Change

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Integrate practice with the rest of a person's life. Treatment is one demand among appointments, care tasks, rest and meaningful activity. Review whether a plan is useful, safe, tolerable and worth its costs; progress need not mean doing more.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R15] Collaborative occupational planning supports discussion of daily-life fit. [R13] A trial's functional and symptom outcomes can differ. [R17] Where ME/CFS is relevant, changing activity requires its condition-specific safeguards. None provides a universal ratio of rest to practice.

Sources: [R15](sources.md#r15), [R13](sources.md#r13), [R17](sources.md#r17). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: A therapy session is followed by transport, food preparation and a family commitment. The plan looked small when the session was considered alone. Work through options: a different appointment time, assistance with a meal, fewer competing demands, or a care-team review. Another person chooses to keep one valued event and asks for help around it. Do not present either choice as morally superior.

Supporter: Agree help and boundaries together. Include a sentence allowing the supporter to say what they can realistically provide, with discussion of other help when available.

Clinician: Review immediate and delayed effects, access, adherence barriers and patient priorities. Worsening can prompt reassessment or treatment modification rather than automatic intensification. Preserve ongoing care and disability support.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional output: “Keep, change, or ask about one part of the current plan.” Success may mean safer participation, easier communication, symptom relief or less burden. Recurrence is not a verdict on effort or personal progress. Refer new/severe changes back to the medical safety material rather than normalizing them as rehabilitation.
