# Research brief — proposed Lesson 16: Automatic Control and Conscious Effort

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Teach automaticity as coordination that does not require deliberate instructions for every component. It is not an on/off switch, a second brain, or evidence of perfect unconscious performance. Introduce three different questions: can an action occur; can it be deliberately initiated now; can it be sustained safely in ordinary life?

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R01] Motor rehabilitation consensus recommends functional tasks and access to automatic movement. This is professional guidance, not proof that conscious monitoring causes every motor symptom. [R02] Selected functional action-tremor participants showed task-dependent effects of attentional focus. Generalization to paralysis, seizures or cognition is unestablished.

Sources: [R01](sources.md#r01), [R02](sources.md#r02). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: A familiar signature usually arrives as a whole action. Having to explain every pen stroke would be a different task. Use this only as an analogy; do not ask the reader to provoke difficulty. Then contrast a brief easier movement with the practical demands of getting dressed every morning. The teaching payoff is permission to ask what conditions make an activity usable, rather than demanding a repeat performance.

Supporter: Someone reaches once for a falling object but later needs help reaching a shelf. Write a conversation about asking whether help is wanted. Do not turn the first movement into evidence against the second difficulty.

Clinician: Explain preserved capacity, initiation, reliability and endurance separately. A positive motor sign contributes to diagnosis in its clinical context; it does not establish safe independent transfers or eliminate coexisting injury.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional question: “Is there a familiar activity I want a therapist to understand better?” A description is sufficient. No strength test, surprise challenge, unsupported standing or equipment withdrawal. Avoid “your automatic brain works, so let it take over.”
