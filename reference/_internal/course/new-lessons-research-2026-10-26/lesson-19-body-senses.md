# Research brief — proposed Lesson 19: Interoception, Proprioception, and Outside Senses

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Explain internal bodily sensing, body position/movement sense and information about the outside world. These categories interact and their scientific boundaries are not perfectly sealed. Separate noticing, interpreting, confidence and measured accuracy; strong sensation is not automatically accurate interpretation, and weak awareness does not invalidate illness.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R05] Interoception includes conscious and unconscious sensing/integration of internal signals. [R06] Proprioception concerns position, movement and force-related senses. [R07] In 44 mixed-FND participants and 48 controls, self-report and some heartbeat-evoked EEG measures differed; heartbeat-counting accuracy did not differ significantly (P=.060). This is cardiac, cross-sectional evidence, not a test of all body senses or treatment efficacy.

Sources: [R05](sources.md#r05), [R06](sources.md#r06), [R07](sources.md#r07). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: In a seated kitchen scene, a person notices hunger, knows where their arm rests, and hears the kettle. Use labels to organize the scene, then ask which information is missing if they feel unwell. Do not tell them to decide the cause from the sensation. Show how a written meal reminder can support daily life without claiming to repair interoception.

Supporter: Accept “something feels wrong” as useful information even without precise vocabulary. Offer words without replacing the person's description.

Clinician: Distinguish task performance, questionnaires, confidence and neural response. Heartbeat tasks have measurement limitations; absence of statistical significance is not proof of equivalence. Assess relevant medical, sensory and medication explanations.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional reflection uses a remembered ordinary scene or the fictional one. Do not prescribe body scans, pulse counting, eyes-closed balance, breath-holding or hyperventilation. The research procedure is not a home exercise.
