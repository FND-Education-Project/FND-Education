# Research brief — proposed Lesson 24: Navigate the Reference Library and Ask Better Questions

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Teach the actual library layout: choose an accessible overview, read “Refers to” and boundaries, choose an audience section, and follow a technique page only when relevant. A page describing a symptom is reading material, not confirmation that the person has it.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R10] Clinical diagnosis needs evidence beyond a reader's resemblance to a description. [R11] Shared decisions and a stated diagnostic rationale are recommended in functional-seizure care. Library-navigation design is a project teaching choice, not a clinically validated intervention.

Sources: [R10](sources.md#r10), [R11](sources.md#r11). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: Work through “my hand shakes while using it.” Open the tremor overview, check what it covers, then locate one question about assessment and one about support. A second worked path starts with fatigue plus headache and reaches co-occurring conditions. Leave room for both paths to matter. End with “What findings support my diagnosis, and which part of this reading applies to me?”

Supporter: Find the supporter section together if invited. Bring one useful question instead of assigning a stack of reading.

Clinician: Show where evidence labels and technical details live. Explain that a diagnostic sign and a treatment technique serve different purposes. Invite correction if the library terminology does not match the patient's experience.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional output: save one relevant page and one question. Alternatives include asking someone to read a paragraph aloud or taking a printed title. Avoid a scavenger hunt requiring many tabs, account creation or extensive memory. Future Reference voice edits may change phrasing; validate links again at publication.
