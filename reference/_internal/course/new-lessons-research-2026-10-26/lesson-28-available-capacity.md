# Research brief — proposed Lesson 28: What Uses Your Available Capacity?

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Use available capacity as everyday planning language. It is not a measured common fuel store. Separate duration, effort, sensory setting, concentration, recovery and valued purpose. A task's visible size does not tell a supporter its personal cost.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R15] OT consensus addresses function, fatigue, cognition and environmental context. [R17] ME/CFS guidance recognizes physical, cognitive, emotional and social activity demands; its management recommendations are condition-specific. These sources do not establish one FND energy mechanism.

Sources: [R15](sources.md#r15), [R17](sources.md#r17). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: Two ten-minute activities differ: reading a complex form in a bright waiting room and resting with a familiar programme at home. Ask what the stopwatch misses. Include a third activity that is enjoyable but still demanding. Explain that enjoyment and effort can coexist; deserving enjoyment is not something the person must earn by finishing chores.

Supporter: Ask which demand is hardest rather than judging from appearance. Include the supporter's own limits in a practical conversation about meals or travel.

Clinician: Clarify what “tired” means to this person: sleepiness, weakness, cognitive strain, pain or delayed worsening may require different assessment. Avoid reducing all limitations to deconditioning.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional output: choose one existing activity and name one demand plus one support. A mental answer is enough. No mandatory scoring, step targets, activity diary or calculation of a biological capacity number. Carry observations into the pacing lesson without assigning a diagnosis.
