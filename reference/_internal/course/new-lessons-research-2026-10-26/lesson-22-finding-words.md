# Research brief — proposed Lesson 22: Finding Words for What Happens

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Apply the existing symptom map rather than create another tracker. Separate what the person felt, what another person saw, what is remembered and what remains unknown. Describe timing and functional effect before selecting a diagnostic label.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R10] FND diagnosis requires appropriate positive clinical evidence and consideration of other conditions. [R11] Seizure guidance recommends patient/witness history, assessment for coexisting epilepsy and video-EEG of typical events where feasible. This does not require every learner to record or film episodes.

Sources: [R10](sources.md#r10), [R11](sources.md#r11). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: “My leg goes funny” becomes “I want to step, but cannot start; I also feel tingling; I do not know whether I am losing strength.” Offer a separate event example: “I remember hearing the first question, then my next memory is sitting down.” Keep unknowns explicitly unknown. A short statement can be useful without becoming a complete case history.

Supporter: “I asked twice and did not hear an answer” reports an observation. “They were unconscious” is an interpretation needing more information. Show the person and supporter placing their accounts beside each other rather than deciding which account wins.

Clinician: Ask about onset, duration, context, awareness, responsiveness, injuries and recovery as appropriate. Questions should not assume psychological triggers or complete recall. New changes deserve proportionate reassessment.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional output: one sentence about an existing familiar experience, with “I do not know” allowed. No compulsory diary, video or reconstruction of missing memories. Do not repeat episodes to obtain a clearer description. Urgent/new-symptom decisions remain with Module 5.
