# Research brief — proposed Lesson 29: Flexible Pacing When Capacity Changes

**October 26, 2026 · Internal authoring material · Not a published lesson**

[Brief index](README.md) · [Source register](sources.md)

## Teaching purpose and explanations to develop

Present pacing as adjusting demands and recovery to the person and circumstances. A boom-and-bust description can identify a pattern without proving why it occurred. Some flares happen despite careful planning. Flexibility includes reducing activity or requesting help, not only expanding activity.

## Researched foundation and its limits

[R17] NICE ME/CFS guidance advises energy management and rejects fixed incremental exercise programmes; it is not an FND-specific pacing trial. Delayed/prolonged worsening needs clinical consideration rather than automatic attribution to ordinary tiredness. [R15] FND activity planning guidance is consensus, not a proven flare-prevention formula.

Sources: [R17](sources.md#r17), [R15](sources.md#r15). See the register for source type, access limits and publication details. These are drafting claims, not a completed independent clinical audit.

## Material for the three audience sections

Person: A fictional better morning leads to laundry, shopping and a visitor on one day. Instead of an ideal timetable, offer choices: share the laundry, shorten the visit, move one task, or preserve today's plan because it matters while discussing support. A second scene worsens despite a modest day, preventing the message that every flare could have been prevented.

Supporter: Negotiate practical help; avoid “I told you not to do that.” The person retains decisions and may have competing needs.

Clinician: Ask about timing, duration, severity and recovery after activity; consider relevant coexisting conditions. If ME/CFS is suspected or established, reconcile rehabilitation advice with its guidance before setting progression.

## Reflection, application and boundaries

Optional question: “What could change on a more difficult day?” Do not prescribe a 10% weekly increase, require symptom provocation, promise that a diary reveals the cause, or imply that keeping activity low is a cure. No one must intentionally trigger delayed worsening to document it.
