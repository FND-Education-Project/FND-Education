## Editorial findings by audience and lesson

- **Person:** Lesson 27's grocery-order humour and concrete retention/transfer scene make neuroplasticity recognizable; short term-definition blockquotes and symptom-diverse examples (voice, motor control, sensation, seizures, cognition, agency) provide needed depth without implying a guaranteed mechanism. Lesson 28 uses actual reported experiences and careful fictional transitions to show variable progress and delayed treatment burden; the six voices are distinct, though denser than earlier lessons. Lesson 29 makes comparative treatment evidence accessible and distinguishes a plausible neurological explanation from proof of an intervention's benefit. All three sections retain **“Something to think about,” “How might that feel?” and “One small step.”** The optional Lesson 29 reflection change above is for scope and cognitive load, not a confirmed scientific correction.
- **Supporter:** Lesson 27's Jess/Amira invitation to demonstrate improved speech and Leah/Natalie's grocery/reminder scenes make **celebration versus performance pressure** visible; Jess is caring, not a caricature. Lesson 28's Daniel/Priya schedule and Natalie's appointment journey show real travel, work, fatigue and relationship constraints without suggesting care should simply stop. Lesson 29's advertised-treatment disagreement and Natalie/Leah's good-humoured reaction to excessive unsolicited advice support consent, autonomy and hope without pressure. All close with two manageable supporter reflection prompts. Natural prose and humour should be preserved.
- **Clinician:** Three **distinct inference tasks** are present. Lesson 27 evaluates **in-session performance vs retention, transfer and safety at home**; Lesson 28 evaluates **total access/appointment burden and sustainable participation**; Lesson 29 evaluates **evidence applicable to the advertised treatment and symptom population**, using rehabilitation and seizure evidence without assuming identical mechanisms. Family doctors participate in wider care coordination, not technique selection. These are illustrative cases; neither Natalie nor another fictional patient should be treated as a study participant.
- **Across audiences:** Progression **what neuroplastic learning is → what rehabilitation experiences can mean → what trial evidence does and does not show** is coherent. Necessary repetition of terminology is usually purposeful. The lesson pages avoid treating disabled people as obliged to improve, reducing every useful adaptation to a cure, or converting one successful task into a universal treatment. Safety is proportionate; retain the distinctions between assessed FND, coexisting migraine, clinical oversight and individual access requirements.
