## Review decision

Keep the approved sequence: **concepts → symptom description/navigation → rehabilitation → pacing**. Two concept modules provide room for examples and application rather than cramming a symptom-producing theory into one lesson. Modules 3 and 4 remain useful foundations: they introduce uncertainty and a personal map; the new modules teach vocabulary in depth, then use that existing map to choose reading and questions.

The syllabus now names the concepts part **Understanding Attention, Action, and Body Signals**. This avoids implying that the taught processes are a proven recipe for producing everyone's symptoms. The reading order is not a treatment order: professional help and accommodations remain available throughout.

**Must address before publication:** persistent-ID collisions; the mismatch between current 75 lessons and account PR #154's 74-lesson allowlist; exact content-equivalence decisions; legacy URLs/deep anchors; evidence attribution and human approval of new lessons. These are future release gates, not changes authorized by this planning PR.

**Worthwhile changes made here:** clearer lesson ownership, narrower attention/interoception claims, specific existing glossary/Reference routes, accurate planning counts, and an internal first draft to calibrate depth and voice.

**Unnecessary:** rewriting human Modules 1–5, reproducing 17 symptom reviews in the course, adding a universal mechanism diagram, creating a glossary link for every word, or writing the remaining 15 new lessons before the first voice/depth checkpoint.

### Module 6

General attention research distinguishes goal-directed selection from capture by important or unexpected information (S1). FND-specific evidence includes a selected functional action-tremor experiment (S2), not a universal test. Motor-rehabilitation consensus supports attention/task-oriented approaches (S3), but a treatment rationale is not proof of causation.

Teach the practical difference between **simplifying competing demands** and **adding another task**. The latter can increase difficulty, especially with cognitive/sensory limitations. Do not equate external focus with doing two things at once. Keep essential symptom reporting and appropriate medical attention available.

### Module 7

Predictive processing is a model/framework, not “thinking symptoms into existence” (S4). Sensing, attention, interpretation, confidence and accuracy are different constructs. Interoception includes processes outside conscious awareness (S5).

A current primary study (S6) compared 44 people with mixed FND with 48 healthy controls. Self-report and some EEG measures differed, while heartbeat-counting accuracy did **not** reach statistical significance. This does not establish normal processing in every domain either. A cardiac task cannot characterize every internal sense, diagnose an individual or prove treatment efficacy. Its breath-holding procedure must not become a course exercise.

Agency is the experience of initiating/controlling an action, distinct from intent, responsibility and sustained ability. The earlier motor study (S7) is phenotype-specific and mechanistic, not proof of a universal FND pathway. Define salience without calling every important signal threatening; arousal is broader than anxiety.

### Module 8

Use paired descriptions with incomplete information: rhythmic versus irregular movement, falling with/without apparent responsiveness, or missing recall versus observed responsiveness. These are **observations**, not a symptom-matching diagnosis. Use the three audience sections on the relevant existing pages, and preserve coexisting-condition routes. Positive diagnosis requires appropriate clinical evidence (S8).

Every scenario should conclude with what remains unknown and a useful question or care step. No “correct FND diagnosis” reveal based on a story. A person need not fill memory gaps, film events or recreate symptoms.

### Module 9

Separate ordinary learning principles, professional consensus and clinical efficacy (S3–S4, S9–S10). Physio4FMD did not show superiority of specialist over usual neurological physiotherapy on its primary physical-function outcome at 12 months; some secondary outcomes favoured specialist treatment. This does not show that all rehabilitation is ineffective. Treatment-response synthesis permits useful outcomes with long-standing symptoms while retaining substantial study limitations.

No general concept proves that a named drill works for every symptom. Continue accommodations and daily-life support. Clinical review, not module order, determines which practice is appropriate.

### Module 10

“Available capacity” is descriptive planning language, not one quantified biological store. FND OT recommendations are consensus, not a validated flare-prevention formula (S11). ME/CFS energy-management guidance applies when that condition is relevant; it is not proof of an FND mechanism (S12). Preserve delayed-worsening assessment, flexible adjustment, basic care, enjoyment and supporter limits.

The Lesson 29 title now says **Flexible Pacing When Capacity Changes**, avoiding an implied promise that pacing prevents every boom-and-bust pattern or flare.

The five updated syllabuses contain exact existing destinations. **Salience and exteroception have no current glossary entries**: define them locally; do not invent fragment links. New entries are a later editorial decision.

Use a few relevant accessible Reference routes per lesson. Technical procedure pages belong primarily in clinician reading. The first internal Person draft uses one practical reading route, rather than a symptom catalogue.
