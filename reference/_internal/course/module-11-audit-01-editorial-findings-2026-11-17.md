## Required editorial-policy and continuity decisions

**R1 — Lesson 34 Clinician, `### Something he noticed in the literature` and `### What has changed in our understanding?` — historically stigmatizing interpretation and research-policy presentation.** The approved narrative discusses Sonoo's 2004 hip-abductor-sign paper and presently reproduces “succeeded in tricking the examiner”, references *hysterical* and links directly to the original paper and central source index **before** giving the modern understanding that FND symptoms are genuine and involuntary. The [research and citation policy](../../../docs/project/research-and-citation-policy.md#diagnostic-evidence-presentation) calls for positive contemporary framing **before** misleading/stigmatizing historical material and requires an essential historical reference's bibliography/ID to appear as **plain text without a public source or central-index hyperlink**, with the original-paper link retained in the internal reading guide. These are concrete *presentation conflicts*, **not a finding that the older study's reported numbers or wording are false**. **Minimum owner-approved approach to discuss:** introduce the contemporary involuntary-symptom framing immediately before the historical example; retain the clinical contrast between a diagnostic test result and an unwarranted inference about patient intentions; change public Sonoo source references to plain-text bibliography/ID and retain the original source link internally. The project lead should decide whether the very short historical quotation is essential to this particular teaching case or should be paraphrased in the public lesson under the existing policy. **Work Audit 2 verifies exact original wording, study groups (reported 16 versus 17 patients), design/blinding, diagnostic performance (reported 33/33) and explanatory limitations before accepting any scientific correction. No clinical claim is certified here.**

**R2 — Lesson 34 Person, `### One small step` — activity does not fully practice Lesson 34's new skill.** The current approved activity asks the reader to notice a familiar symptom and consider nervous-system processes, which closely revisits **Lesson 33**. Lesson 34 actually teaches **clarifying what an offered referral will answer and making sure the original healthcare question remains addressed**. **Recommended minimal replacement, subject to owner approval:** “Think of one question you would like a healthcare professional to answer. If useful, write it down in a few words, such as ‘What is this referral meant to help with?’ You don't need an upcoming appointment to do this.” Retain the approved `Something to think about` and `How might that feel?` questions, the entire Nadia story, and all other material. This is a **lesson-specific alignment improvement**, not a general rewrite.

**R3 — Module identity, lesson titles and routing — reconcile old generated material against owner-approved new curriculum.** The approved story and uploaded crossword titles are **Lesson 33: “Recognizing How Far You've Come”; Lesson 34: “Working With Your Healthcare Professionals”; Lesson 35: “Finding Your Way Through Healthcare Barriers.”** However the public lesson H1 headings and opening summaries still use the previous generic treatment-team plan (“Who May Be on the Treatment Team”, “Shared Goals and Clear Responsibilities”, “Care When No Local FND Specialist Is Available”), the folder/module README is titled **“Building an Individual Treatment Team”**, its generated breadcrumbs refer to **Module 13**, and the existing detailed syllabus `docs/project/syllabus/module-11-pain-migraine-fatigue-and-sleep.md` describes a **different old Module 11**; the generated `docs/project/syllabus/module-13-building-an-individual-treatment-team.md` also reflects the legacy plan. **Project lead should confirm the three crossword titles as the final public H1/navigation titles and choose the replacement Module 11 overview title** (suggestion: **“Recognizing Progress and Navigating Healthcare”**). **Work Audit 2 should then implement consistent headings, introduction, module overview, syllabus/course sequence, metadata/links/breadcrumbs and course/progress-tracker tests without rewriting human-authored body prose or renaming stable lesson URLs/IDs absent specific approval.** In this audit no navigation or syllabus was silently changed.
