> **Editorial boundary for reviewers:** The online course is the main place for broad FND education. The booklets briefly repeat only what a person needs in order to use a standalone functional-seizure exercise safely, then add seizure-specific practice.

## The simple rule

- **Course:** explain shared FND concepts fully and provide the broad clinical and safety context.
- **Functional-seizure reference pages:** compare evidence, programmes, resources, and practical options.
- **Booklets:** help the reader apply, practise, record, and personalize seizure-specific skills.

Readers should be able to print one booklet and understand it without being forced to complete the entire course first. That requires purposeful repetition, but not a second full FND course.

## Statements repeated in every booklet where relevant

The wording can be shortened, but these ideas must remain visible without relying on another page:

- Functional seizures are real and involuntary.
- They are not epileptic seizures, but a person can have both.
- No single cause or psychological history is required.
- An exercise is for an established, assessed pattern; it cannot diagnose an episode.
- A strategy that does not help has not been “failed” by the person.
- New, severe, injured, or substantially changed events require the person's agreed medical plan or reassessment.
- General education cannot decide an individual's driving, water, height, machinery, medication, or emergency rules.

## Material not repeated in full

The booklets will link back to the course for the complete discussion of diagnostic signs, differential diagnosis, causation debates, all FND symptom types, medication principles, multidisciplinary roles, accessibility law, benefits or disability systems, and the general evidence for psychological therapy. Repeating those topics in depth would make the booklets longer, less focused, and harder to update safely.

## How repetition should look

Use a one-page “What you need before this booklet” illustrated recap, a margin safety symbol, and short links or QR codes to the fuller course pages. Do not use “As you already learned,” because some readers will start with a booklet. Write the essential statement once in plain language, then move directly to the seizure-specific example or worksheet.
