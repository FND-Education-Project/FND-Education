# Unified CBT-Informed Booklets for Functional Seizures

> **Five-booklet production outline:** Each linked page is a separate, reviewer-ready outline. The final booklets are intended for reading on screen or downloading and printing. They are education and guided self-reflection, not a substitute for individualized therapy or medical care.

## The five booklets

1. [Understanding Functional Seizures and CBT](01-understanding-functional-seizures-and-cbt.md)
2. [Discovering Your Seizure Pattern](02-discovering-your-seizure-pattern.md)
3. [Skills for Warnings and Seizure Onset](03-skills-for-warnings-and-seizure-onset.md)
4. [Returning to Activities and Independence](04-returning-to-activities-and-independence.md)
5. [Support, Setbacks, and Maintaining Progress](05-support-setbacks-and-maintaining-progress.md)

The order is deliberate: understand the diagnosis and treatment idea; build a personal map; try carefully chosen skills; regain parts of life; then make progress durable. A clinician may change the order or omit activities to match the person.

## Intended readers

The primary version is for adults with an established functional-seizure diagnosis. Supporter boxes are included throughout. A later youth adaptation should use paediatric evidence, safeguarding review, school examples, and developmentally suitable language rather than merely changing the pictures.

## Standard design for every booklet

- **Target length:** approximately 12–16 pages, including worksheets; one main idea per page or two-page spread.
- **Reading level:** plain, respectful language; define CBT, formulation, avoidance, dissociation, and exposure when first used.
- **Navigation:** “Learn,” “Try,” “Personalize,” “Safety,” and “Take to an appointment” markers used consistently.
- **Print:** A4 and US Letter safe margins, black-and-white legibility, minimal background ink, no information communicated by colour alone.
- **Digital:** tagged PDF, real headings, logical reading order, selectable text, descriptive links, form fields where practical, and alt text for every meaningful image.
- **Cognitive access:** short paragraphs, generous white space, large writing areas, worked examples, optional low-demand versions, and a one-page summary.
- **Representation:** varied ages, body types, mobility aids, cultures, genders, homes, schools, and workplaces; avoid showing one “typical” seizure appearance.
- **Tone:** collaborative and curious. Exercises ask “What happened?” and “What might we test?” rather than “What did you do wrong?”

## Common page pattern

1. Cover and one-sentence purpose.
2. “What you need before this booklet”: brief diagnosis and safety recap.
3. Two or three teaching spreads with diagrams and ordinary-life examples.
4. One worked example clearly labelled as fictional.
5. One or two large-print worksheets.
6. “Why this may help” and “What the evidence cannot tell us.”
7. Lower-demand and accessibility options.
8. Safety and professional-support box.
9. One-page personal summary that can join a personal FND handbook.
10. Sources, version, and review date.

## Visual language

Use original illustrations rather than decorative stock images. Useful recurring devices include:

- **route maps** for sequences and choice points;
- **loops** for self-reinforcing patterns, always with more than one possible exit;
- **dimmer switches** for graded change rather than on/off success;
- **weather maps** for changing internal and external conditions, without implying that emotions are the sole cause;
- **tool cards** showing one skill and when it may fit;
- **comic panels** for supporter dialogue and ordinary-life experiments;
- **before/during/after strips** for episodes and recovery; and
- **progress dashboards** showing several outcomes, not seizure count alone.

Never use a broken-brain image, puppet strings, theatrical masks, a person “choosing” to fall, or red-alert imagery that could increase fear. Never copy a figure from an existing manual unless its licence and attribution requirements explicitly allow adaptation.

## Evidence wording used in the booklets

Prefer: “This may help you…,” “This is one explanation you can test,” and “This skill was included in a studied treatment programme.” Avoid: “CBT works by…,” “Your seizures happen because…,” and “If you practise, you will stop having seizures.” See [the evidence and unification method](../evidence-and-unification-method.md).

## What completion produces

The worksheets are designed to accumulate rather than disappear into separate booklets. By the end, the reader may have:

- a concise diagnosis and question page;
- a personal before–during–after map;
- two or three tested skill cards;
- a graded participation plan;
- an agreed supporter and recovery response; and
- a setback and maintenance page.

These pages can form the functional-seizure section of the person's broader FND handbook.

## Review sequence before PDF production

1. Clinical and evidence review, including adult and paediatric separation.
2. Lived-experience review by people with different seizure patterns, including no warning and persistent seizures.
3. Supporter review that preserves consent and autonomy.
4. Trauma-informed and anti-stigma language review.
5. Accessibility, cognitive-load, screen-reader, and low-ink print review.
6. Copyright and image-licensing review.
7. Small usability test: can a reader explain what is being taught, why it may help, how to try it, and when to stop?
