# Symptom-Specific Diagnostic Signs

> **Working draft:** This reference was automatically generated and is looking for contributors and reviewers.

This reference explains how clinicians may gather **positive evidence** for particular Functional Neurological Disorder (FND) symptom presentations. Each symptom page describes the examination, observation, test or diagnostic criteria used; what the finding can support; what it cannot establish; and how a contributor could demonstrate the method with an accessible image or video.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These pages are not self-diagnosis instructions. A sign must be chosen and interpreted by a suitably qualified clinician in the context of the person’s history, full examination, possible coexisting conditions and appropriate testing.

## Why some terms share one page

Symptoms are grouped only when the cited clinical literature treats them as one diagnostic presentation:

- **Weakness and paralysis** are covered under the research term *functional limb weakness*.
- **Jerks and myoclonus** share a page because *myoclonus* is the clinical term for brief jerk-like movements and the cited neurophysiology literature assesses them together.
- **Dystonia and fixed posturing** share a page because fixed posturing is a recognized functional dystonia presentation.
- **Speech and voice** share a page under the published category *functional communication disorders*.
- **Swallowing symptoms and globus**, and **cough and related upper-airway symptoms**, follow the categories used in the speech-and-language-therapy consensus paper.
- The dizziness page is limited to **persistent postural-perceptual dizziness (PPPD)**. It must not be used as a template for diagnosing every kind of dizziness.

Visual symptoms remain separate from ordinary touch, pain, temperature and position-sense symptoms because their assessment requires ophthalmic and neuro-ophthalmic methods. Functional seizures remain separate from jerks because seizure assessment depends on the complete event and often video-EEG, not a movement sign viewed in isolation.

## Standard for every technique page

Each page should answer:

1. Which exact symptom presentation is being assessed?
2. What positive feature, test or criteria set is being demonstrated?
3. Who is qualified to perform and interpret it?
4. What preparation and equipment are needed?
5. What sequence should a media contributor record?
6. What finding may support the diagnosis?
7. What alternative explanations or confounders must be considered?
8. What does the finding **not** prove?
9. What safety, consent, privacy and accessibility protections are required?
10. Which research or clinical guidance supports the page?

## Media contributor requirements

The person appearing in real clinical media must give informed, recorded permission for the agreed use. Consent to medical care does not automatically include consent to public filming. The person must be able to pause or withdraw from filming without affecting their care.

Every contribution should:

- be planned with a clinician qualified to perform the technique;
- use a real examination only when it is already clinically appropriate—never provoke symptoms solely to obtain footage;
- identify whether the participant is a patient, clinician, actor or healthy volunteer;
- show the full task before using close-ups, arrows or slow motion;
- preserve the sequence without edits that could create a false comparison;
- explain what the sign supports and what it cannot prove;
- avoid language about catching, tricking, testing effort or proving that symptoms are voluntary;
- avoid faces and identifying details unless they are necessary and specifically authorized;
- provide captions, a transcript and descriptive alternative text;
- avoid flashing, autoplay, abrupt loud sound and unnecessary motion;
- include a written version that remains complete when the media cannot be viewed.

Suggested media files should eventually be stored under `assets/media/diagnostic-signs/`, with descriptive filenames matching the symptom page.

## Subjects not yet included

This first index does not create technique pages for every reported FND-associated symptom. Hearing changes, bladder symptoms, breathing-pattern disorders, dissociation and several less common movement presentations need a clearer symptom-specific research package before technique instructions are added. A missing page does not mean that the symptom is unimportant or cannot be functional.
