# The Biopsychosocial Model and Misconceptions

> **Working draft:** This page was automatically generated and is looking for [contributors and reviewers](https://fndeducationproject.org/contact/).

The **biopsychosocial model** is a way to consider how health, experiences and living conditions interact. It is a planning framework, not a test for FND and not proof that psychological factors caused an individual's symptoms. (*citations* [1](#citation-1), [2](#citation-2))

## For the Person With FND
{: #for-the-person-with-fnd .shareable }

### Three kinds of influence

The biopsychosocial model invites clinicians to look at more than one part of a person's life:

- **Biological:** neurological symptoms, pain, sleep, other illnesses, physical limitations and treatment effects.
- **Psychological:** attention, learning, expectations, feelings, coping strategies and, where relevant, mental-health conditions.
- **Social:** family support, healthcare access, relationships, work demands, finances, stigma and the environment.

These categories overlap. For instance, poor sleep can change pain and attention, and a noisy environment can make communication difficult. Such interactions are not unique to FND.

### What the model does not mean

The model **does not diagnose FND**. It cannot tell a clinician that a person's symptoms are caused by trauma, depression or anxiety. Many people with FND report no clear psychological trigger, and an FND diagnosis does not require a psychiatric diagnosis. A clinician must still identify appropriate positive features of FND and investigate other conditions when indicated. (*citations* [1](#citation-1), [2](#citation-2))

It is also not an excuse to dismiss pain or other symptoms as “just stress.” A person may have a useful psychological or practical treatment without that treatment proving a psychological origin. Treatment can help change a current difficulty even when the original cause is unknown.

### Different questions deserve different answers

It can help to separate these questions:

1. **What supports the diagnosis?** Clinical history and suitable positive neurological features.
2. **What contributed to the symptoms beginning?** The answer may be uncertain or different for different people.
3. **What makes everyday life harder or easier now?** This might include sleep, pain, accessibility, communication, work demands or supportive rehabilitation.

Answering the third question can improve care without pretending the second has been solved. This is a practical way to use the biopsychosocial framework while respecting scientific uncertainty. (*citations* [2](#citation-2), [3](#citation-3))

### Misunderstandings you may encounter

A functional symptom is not the same as a deliberately produced symptom. Its fluctuations do not prove that a person is exaggerating. Not having a visible lesion on a particular scan does not make a symptom unreal. And a referral for psychological therapy does not mean other medical care or rehabilitation is unnecessary.

### Questions to reflect on

Which parts of daily life affect what you can do now? Are these influences being mistaken for an explanation of how your FND began?

### One small step

Think of one practical change that would make tomorrow more manageable, such as less sensory load, more help with a task, clearer communication or time to recover after an appointment. This is an experiment in meeting needs, not a test of whether you can control symptoms.

## For Family, Friends, and Other Supporters
{: #for-family-friends-and-other-supporters .shareable }

A person does not need to disclose trauma or identify an emotional cause before receiving support. Avoid treating anxiety, conflict or symptom changes as evidence that they are producing symptoms on purpose. Ask what the person wants help with and respect their choice about treatment, conversations and privacy.

A useful supporter can distinguish an offer of help from pressure to recover on a particular timetable. When symptoms change significantly or new concerns arise, support appropriate medical reassessment rather than assuming they are part of a known pattern.

## For Clinicians and the Care Team
{: #for-clinicians-and-the-care-team .shareable }

Differentiate diagnosis, suspected predisposing or precipitating influences, currently modifiable barriers and goals of care. The biopsychosocial framework can structure an individualized formulation; it is neither a validated biomarker nor a universally established causal model. Avoid inferring trauma, psychiatric disease or intentional symptom production from the FND diagnosis itself.

Ask about function, participation, supportive relationships, accessibility, disability and coexisting conditions rather than focusing only on symptom severity. Explain proposed mechanisms as hypotheses where appropriate; treatments and social supports should be offered through collaborative decisions. (*citations* [1](#citation-1), [2](#citation-2), [3](#citation-3))
