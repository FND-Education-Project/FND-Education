# Module 9 — Rehabilitation and Neuroplasticity

**Part Three: Applying What You Have Learned**  
**Status:** Approved curriculum direction; lessons, evidence audit and clinical review pending.  
**Moves and refines:** Former [Module 14 — Rehabilitation and Neuroplastic Change](module-14-rehabilitation-and-neuroplastic-change.md).

## Purpose

Put attention, automatic control, prediction and agency into a rehabilitation context, while explaining *neuroplasticity without promises of cure*. Keep access, safety and quality of life as outcomes alongside symptom improvement.

## Proposed focused lessons

### Lesson 25 — How Rehabilitation Uses Learning
- Neuroplasticity means nervous systems can adapt with experience, but does not guarantee recovery.
- Describe retraining through attention, automaticity, practice and meaningful tasks without claiming these mechanisms explain every symptom.
- Show the role of appropriately trained physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech-language therapy and other care.
- Explain why a good response does not imply an earlier symptom was voluntary.

### Lesson 26 — Choosing and Adjusting Meaningful Practice
- Begin with a personally meaningful activity and the function needed for it.
- Agree safe adaptations, manageable practice and what to monitor or stop; professional guidance for hazardous activities.
- Distinguish useful consistency from coercive repetition or pushing through injury and delayed worsening.
- Accommodation and rehabilitation may coexist. Equipment need not be withheld pending improvement.

### Lesson 27 — Evidence, Expectations and Treatment Claims
- What research actually supports, uncertainty and individual variation.
- Distinguish a therapeutic model from evidence that a particular intervention works.
- Evaluate “rewire your brain” claims, promised timelines, adverse effects and conflicts of interest.
- Track safety, participation, effort and quality of life, not only symptom frequency.

## Supporter perspective

Encourage without supervising every exercise, testing performance or using shame. Respect fatigue, access aids, consent and agreed goals.

## Clinician perspective

Shared goal-setting, safe transfer into meaningful activities, consistent communication of positive diagnostic evidence, proportionate practice and adverse-effect monitoring. Address coexisting conditions and access needs, including where specialist services are limited.

## Outcome

Learners understand rehabilitation as an individualized learning process and can ask informed questions about benefits, effort, risks, alternatives and uncertainty.

**Boundary:** Specific motor, seizure, speech, sensory and other intervention instructions remain in the three-audience [Recovery Reference](../../../reference/recovery-techniques/README.md), subject to appropriate clinical assessment.
