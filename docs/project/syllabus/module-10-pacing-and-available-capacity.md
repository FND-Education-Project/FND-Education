# Module 10 — Pacing, Activity, and Available Capacity

**Part Three: Applying What You Have Learned**<br>
**Status:** Approved curriculum direction; planning evidence reviewed October 26, 2026; full lesson audit and clinical review pending.<br>
**Moves and refines:** Former [Module 15 — Pacing, Activity, and the Boom-and-Bust Cycle](module-15-pacing-activity-and-the-boom-and-bust-cycle.md).

## Purpose

Teach practical decision-making when movement, attention, sensory tolerance, energy and recovery vary. Build on the introductory capacity discussion in Module 1 and the concepts/rehabilitation modules without promising that careful pacing will resolve symptoms.

## Proposed focused lessons

### Lesson 28 — What Uses Your Available Capacity?
- Physical, cognitive, sensory, emotional and social activities may each carry costs.
- Capacity can vary by task, setting and time; externally invisible effort still counts.
- Rest is not a moral failure; participation and enjoyable activities count.
- Distinguish observation of a pattern from assuming a specific cause.

### Lesson 29 — Flexible Pacing When Capacity Changes
- Define boom-and-bust and limitations of an inflexible schedule.
- Consider ranges for more-capable and less-capable days without overtracking.
- Preserve accommodations, recovery opportunities and meaningful chosen activities.
- Avoid routine encouragement to push through delayed or prolonged deterioration; assess post-exertional symptom exacerbation and coexisting conditions appropriately.

### Lesson 30 — Balancing Rehabilitation, Rest, and Change
- Practice, daily tasks and recreation share time and can add to the total demands a person manages. Capacity is descriptive; this is not a claim that all activities draw on one measured biological store.
- Collaboratively review whether activity is sustainable, useful and safe.
- Adjust plans after setbacks without attaching blame or promising steady improvement.
- Use simple examples rather than exhaustive tracking schedules.

## Supporter perspective

Support negotiated plans without becoming an activity police officer. Balance practical help with supporter capacity.

## Clinician perspective

Individualize for delayed worsening, migraine, sleep disorders, pain, dysautonomia, ME/CFS overlap, functional disability and risk; explain evidence limitations for FND-specific pacing approaches.

## Outcome

Learner can reason about sustainable activity, sensible recovery and changing needs without assuming a fixed prescription fits everyone.

## Reviewed evidence and practical routes

The [Work review](../../../reference/_internal/course/syllabus-redesign-review-2026-10-26.md#module-10) distinguishes FND occupational-therapy consensus from ME/CFS guidance and uncertain pacing trials. A boom-and-bust description must not imply that every flare was preventable or caused by a poor activity choice. Capacity concerns can require assessment of another condition.

Use [available capacity](../../../glossary/README.md#available-capacity), [pacing](../../../glossary/README.md#pacing), and [fatigue/post-activity worsening](../../../reference/co-occurring-conditions/06-fatigue.md) at their useful first appearances. Preserve choices, assistance, enjoyment and recovery time, including when no increase in activity is possible.
