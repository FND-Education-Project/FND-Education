# Evidence Standard

Every substantial educational page will distinguish among the following kinds of information.

## 1. Established or well-supported evidence

Findings supported by comparatively strong research, replicated findings, major reviews, or established diagnostic evidence.

## 2. Clinical consensus or guideline-based practice

Recommendations supported by professional guidelines, consensus documents, or established specialist practice, even when randomized-trial evidence remains incomplete.

## 3. Emerging or uncertain evidence

Promising findings that remain preliminary, inconsistent, limited, or under active investigation.

## 4. Clinical model or hypothesis

A proposed explanation or framework that may be useful but should not be presented as established biological fact.

## 5. Lived experience and community-reported practice

Experiences, adaptations, and self-management ideas reported by people living with FND or those who support them. These reports may provide valuable practical ideas where formal research is sparse, but they do not prove that an approach works, is safe for everyone, or caused a reported improvement.

## Applying the standard

- Identify disagreement and uncertainty rather than hiding it.
- Distinguish statistical association from causation.
- Describe who was studied and whether the findings are likely to generalize.
- Note important limitations, including sample size, comparison groups, study design, and conflicts of interest where relevant.
- Do not turn the absence of evidence into proof that something does not work.
- Do not turn a plausible explanation into established fact.
- Date evidence reviews so readers can see how current they are.
