# Our Role

FND Education exists to explain and organize information, not to become an authority on Functional Neurological Disorder (FND).

The authority should remain with identifiable sources: published research, clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, consensus documents, and clearly identified clinical expertise. Our role is to find that material, examine it critically, explain it in ordinary language, connect it to questions people with FND and their supporters actually have, and make the original sources easy to find.

## How the project should speak

The wording should show where a claim comes from and how certain it is. Appropriate phrases include:

- “Research has found…”
- “Several studies suggest…”
- “A systematic review reported…”
- “Clinical guidelines recommend…”
- “Researchers do not yet know…”
- “There is disagreement about…”
- “This is a proposed model rather than an established explanation…”

Categorical statements such as “FND is caused by…,” “This happens because…,” or “The correct treatment is…” should be used only when the evidence genuinely supports that certainty.

Readers should be able to tell where an idea came from, how strong the evidence is, and where present knowledge ends.


## Information for clinicians and care teams

The clinician sections are intended to make evidence practically useful in patient care. They should help a clinician explain the subject and the patient’s individual presentation, assess what may help, support symptom or functional improvement, and remain helpful when improvement is limited or not yet possible. They should preserve diagnostic reasoning, safety, uncertainty, and reassessment while keeping the patient’s understanding, function, autonomy, accessibility, and quality of life at the centre.
