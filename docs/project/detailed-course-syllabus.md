## Living With FND: Knowledge, Skills, and Support

This document is the working outline for the full FND Education course. It identifies what each module is intended to cover; it is not itself finished educational content.

The course will be self-paced. Readers may follow it in order or enter through a symptom-specific pathway. Every module will include material for the person with FND and the people who support them.

Each module will separate **essential knowledge**, written in very plain language for a wide audience, from **professional and advanced detail**. A reader should not need medical training—or need to read the advanced section—to understand the central lesson and use its practical information.

## Course-wide outcomes

By the end of the course, a person with FND should be better able to:

- explain what research and clinical guidance say FND is—and what remains uncertain;
- understand how clinicians make a positive diagnosis;
- distinguish primary FND symptoms, associated symptoms, separate diagnoses, and meaningful medical changes;
- identify research-supported, guideline-based, and lived-experience-informed management ideas;
- participate in treatment and rehabilitation decisions;
- communicate needs, boundaries, and safety plans;
- build a personal reference for episodes, appointments, setbacks, and daily life.

A support person should be better able to:

- understand that functional symptoms are described as genuine and involuntary;
- respond calmly and safely without blame or overprotection;
- support independence, rehabilitation, communication, and medical reassessment;
- recognize the limits of the support-person role;
- protect their own health and the underlying relationship.

A clinician or care-team member should be better able to:

- explain the subject and the patient’s own presentation clearly and respectfully;
- assess what may help that individual rather than only confirm a label;
- support symptom improvement, function, safety, participation, and quality of life;
- remain useful when improvement is limited or not yet possible;
- recognize when new information, non-response, harm, or clinical change requires reassessment.

# Course Modules — Proposed 19-Module Curriculum

**Curriculum approved for planning: October 26, 2026.** This is the target syllabus, **not** the live site structure. The current course continues to use 23 numbered module folders and its established lesson URLs, lesson progress IDs, search results and puzzles until a separately verified migration. New module numbers below are *proposed*. [Work implementation and migration handoff](../../reference/_internal/course/syllabus-redesign-handoff-2026-10-26.md).

Our teaching pathway is: **FND foundations → safety → understanding attention and body-brain processes → describing and distinguishing symptoms → rehabilitation → pacing → broader treatment and everyday life → longer-term management**.

## Part One — Understanding FND

The first five modules remain in their established order, with no rewrite implied by this syllabus change.

1. [**Module 1 — What FND Is**](syllabus/module-01-what-fnd-is.md) — Involuntary symptoms, introductory explanations, recovery and available capacity.
2. [**Module 2 — How FND Is Diagnosed**](syllabus/module-02-how-fnd-is-diagnosed.md) — Positive signs, investigation, other conditions and uncertainty.
3. [**Module 3 — Causes, Mechanisms, and Honest Uncertainty**](syllabus/module-03-causes-mechanisms-and-honest-uncertainty.md) — Introduce the models and research without implying an individual cause.
4. [**Module 4 — Mapping Your Individual Condition**](syllabus/module-04-mapping-your-individual-condition.md) — Organize diagnoses, open questions, possible warnings and useful supports.
5. [**Module 5 — Medical Safety and New Symptoms**](syllabus/module-05-medical-safety-and-new-symptoms.md) — Individual safety plans and deciding when new or changed symptoms need medical assessment.

## Part Two — Understanding How Symptoms Can Happen

Teach nervous-system concepts deeply enough for readers to reason about adaptations. Explain normal nervous-system processes and FND research without claiming an established universal symptom-production mechanism.

6. [**Module 6 — Attention and Automatic Control**](syllabus/module-06-attention-and-automatic-control.md) — Selective and divided attention; internal/external focus; automatic movement, conscious effort, distraction, context and variability. *Planned lessons 15–17.*
7. [**Module 7 — Prediction, Body Signals, and Agency**](syllabus/module-07-prediction-interoception-and-agency.md) — Predictions and evidence; interoception, proprioception and exteroception; salience, arousal, feedback, agency and adaptable practice. *Planned lessons 18–21.*
8. [**Module 8 — Recognizing Symptoms and Finding Information**](syllabus/module-08-recognizing-symptoms-and-finding-information.md) — Describe features of symptoms, compare superficially similar experiences without self-diagnosing, and follow the three-audience Reference Library. *Planned lessons 22–24.*

The earlier symptom-by-symptom Module 6–12 drafts are superseded as the *planned course structure*, but their content and URLs remain intact pending review and migration. Symptom-specific diagnosis, rehabilitation methods and detailed care belong primarily in [Diagnostic Signs](../../reference/diagnostic-signs/README.md), [Recovery Techniques](../../reference/recovery-techniques/README.md) and [Co-occurring Conditions](../../reference/co-occurring-conditions/README.md).

## Part Three — Applying What You Have Learned

Rehabilitation and pacing come directly after the concepts and symptom-navigation teaching.

9. [**Module 9 — Rehabilitation and Neuroplasticity**](syllabus/module-09-rehabilitation-and-neuroplasticity.md) — Individualized learning, practice, adaptation, evaluating evidence and avoiding cure promises. *Previously Module 14; planned lessons 25–27.*
10. [**Module 10 — Pacing, Activity, and Available Capacity**](syllabus/module-10-pacing-and-available-capacity.md) — Physical, cognitive and sensory load, flexible pacing, rehabilitation/rest balance and delayed worsening. *Previously Module 15; planned lessons 28–30.*

## Part Four — Treatment and Living With FND

**Keep the following subjects for now**, including their existing detail and possible overlap. Their linked syllabus files still carry their **legacy module numbers**; Work will create/rename approved files and move lesson content during a separate migration.

11. **Building Your Individual Treatment Team** — Care roles, coordination and gaps where specialists are unavailable. [Existing Module 13 syllabus](syllabus/module-13-building-an-individual-treatment-team.md).
12. **Psychological Treatment Without Blame** — Therapy options, consent, evidence and the difference between mental-health support and assigning a cause to FND. [Existing Module 16 syllabus](syllabus/module-16-psychological-treatment-without-blame.md).
13. **Daily Living, Accessibility, and Equipment** — Adapt tasks and surroundings; fit mobility and communication aids; protect current access. [Existing Module 17 syllabus](syllabus/module-17-daily-living-accessibility-and-equipment.md).
14. **Relationships, Identity, and Grief** — Changing roles, boundaries, shared life, supporter wellbeing and meaningful connection. [Existing Module 18 syllabus](syllabus/module-18-relationships-identity-and-grief.md).
15. **Healthcare Communication and Self-Advocacy** — Appointments, records, memory aids, decisions and follow-up. [Existing Module 19 syllabus](syllabus/module-19-healthcare-communication-and-self-advocacy.md).
16. **Work, Disability, and Community Participation** — Accessibility, rights, realistic participation, changing function and meaning beyond employment. [Existing Module 20 syllabus](syllabus/module-20-work-disability-and-community-participation.md).

## Part Five — Long-Term Management

17. **Setbacks, Relapse, and Changing Symptoms** — Respond to fluctuation without blame or missed medical change. [Existing Module 21 syllabus](syllabus/module-21-setbacks-relapse-and-changing-symptoms.md).
18. **Building Your Personal FND Handbook** — Usable information that reduces memory load and supports safe care. [Existing Module 22 syllabus](syllabus/module-22-building-your-personal-fnd-handbook.md).
19. **Reviewing Progress and Choosing Next Steps** — Assess confidence, participation, safety, quality of life and supporter needs alongside symptoms. [Existing Module 23 syllabus](syllabus/module-23-reviewing-progress.md).

For content migration, crosswalk, safety checks, audits and learner account preservation, see the [approved redesign handoff](../../reference/_internal/course/syllabus-redesign-handoff-2026-10-26.md).

## Support-Person Mini-Course

### Lesson 1 — Believe, Listen, and Learn

- evidence and guidance concerning genuine, involuntary symptoms;
- listening to the person’s account;
- avoiding interrogation, blame, and unsolicited causal theories;
- validation without unrealistic promises.

### Lesson 2 — Safe Help During Episodes

- individualized episode plans;
- injury reduction and observation;
- emergency criteria;
- calm recovery support;
- actions to avoid.

### Lesson 3 — Supporting Rehabilitation

- independence and consent;
- agreed cues and strategies;
- encouraging without pressuring;
- helping without becoming the therapist;
- adapting when capacity changes.

### Lesson 4 — Communication and Cognitive Support

- written reminders and summaries;
- one subject or instruction at a time;
- additional processing time;
- alternative communication;
- supporting decisions without removing autonomy.

### Lesson 5 — Boundaries and Caregiver Health

- recognizing overload and burnout;
- sharing responsibilities;
- realistic limits and respite;
- safety for both people;
- preserving the underlying relationship.

### Lesson 6 — Planning Together

- episode and emergency plans;
- appointment roles;
- daily support preferences;
- privacy and consent;
- setback plans;
- regular review and revision.

## Find Information by Symptom or Need

Use proposed Module 8 for learning how to describe and compare features, then navigate the [Reference Library](../../reference/README.md). Its three-audience [diagnostic](../../reference/diagnostic-signs/README.md), [recovery](../../reference/recovery-techniques/README.md), and [co-occurring conditions](../../reference/co-occurring-conditions/README.md) pages are the detailed source for particular symptoms. These reference entries will receive separate human-voice editing after the course.

The course does not diagnose by symptom matching and does not replace new-symptom or emergency assessment. Follow the safety teaching in Module 5.

## Standard Components of Each Module

The current live module folders contain (retain while migration is being designed):

- a short `README.md` overview that introduces the module and links to its focused pages;
- one to five focused pages, giving each module between two and six total pages;
- stand-alone focused pages that remain understandable when reached directly from search;
- one canonical home for each full explanation, with other pages summarizing and linking rather than duplicating it.

The current drafted Parts Two through Five use a lower-reading-load pattern; preserve the useful elements in the proposed course: one main idea at a time, a plain-language definition beside an illustration, a short essential message, personal reflection questions, and a small optional action. Practical and safety-sensitive topics have their own pages so definitions, decisions and daily-life guidance do not have to be absorbed in one sitting.

Each future focused lesson must continue using four visible sections:

1. **For the Person With FND**
2. **For Family, Friends, and Other Supporters**
3. **For Clinicians and the Care Team**
4. **Research and Sources**

Within those sections, the existing 15 recurring authoring questions preserve the original page requirements: terminology, short summary, research findings, uncertainty, lived presentation, at-home ideas, personal reflection questions, related reading, supporter guidance, clinician explanation, individualized assessment, support for improvement, continuing support when improvement is limited, evidence notes, and last-reviewed date. Differential diagnosis, safety-netting, and medical reassessment remain within the clinician questions rather than disappearing from the page.
